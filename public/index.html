Gmail

Works on your phone. PackTrack reads your shipping emails straight from Gmail (read-only) and adds the packages it finds. Your mail never goes anywhere else.

One-time setup (about 10 minutes, easiest on a computer)

Google only lets apps read Gmail with a sign-in key (“client ID”) that you create for yourself. It's free.

  1. Go to console.cloud.google.com, sign in with your Gmail account and create a new project (call it PackTrack).
  2. Search for Gmail API in the top bar, open it and tap Enable.
  3. Open Google Auth Platform (or APIs & Services → OAuth consent screen) → Get started. App name PackTrack, your email as support email, audience External, then finish.
  4. Under Audience → Test users, add your own Gmail address.
  5. Under Clients (or Credentials) → Create client → type Web application. Add:
    Authorized JavaScript origin:
    Authorized redirect URI:
    Then Create.
  6. Copy the Client ID (ends in .apps.googleusercontent.com) and paste it in PackTrack Settings → Google client ID.
  7. Tap Connect Gmail. Google will say “Google hasn't verified this app”. That's expected for your own app, so tap Continue.

Google signs you out of the app after an hour. PackTrack checks Gmail automatically while you're signed in; after that, tap Check Gmail now to sign in again with one tap.

Other email providers (needs a computer)

For Outlook and other mailboxes, run the small PackTrack helper on your computer. It reads the mailbox (read-only) and hands the packages it finds to PackTrack.

  1. Download the project, then put your mail settings in .env (see .env.example).
  2. Run npm install and npm start.
  3. Enter the helper address (usually http://localhost:3000) in Settings.

Settings

Live tracking

PackTrack gets live status from a tracking aggregator that covers 1,000+ carriers (Posti, Matkahuolto, PostNord, DHL, UPS, FedEx, USPS, Cainiao, YunExpress…). All three have free tiers — add a key for at least one. Without a key you can still store packages and open the carrier's tracking page.
